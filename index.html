Executive Summary

The project in one page

MRP Team 18 developed a Youth AI Impact Monitoring Dashboard for Eastbrook City to address a critical governance gap. The city wanted to expand AI programs for its young residents, but had no data on whether current AI use was healthy or harmful.

Analysis of a 1,500-record synthetic dataset covering 22 behavioral and physical variables shows that 64.8% of youth decisions are AI-assisted, the average verification rate is only 1.12 out of 10, and 486 youth cannot make a decision at all without AI. Average screen time is 154 minutes per day, driving documented eye strain and physical stiffness.

Three computed KPI scores quantify the problem. The AI Dependence Score sits at 14.29, the Physical Wellness Risk Index at 0.29, and the Decision Autonomy Rate at just 1.50. After a 12-month intervention cycle, the model projects a 44% drop in AI dependence, a 48% improvement in physical wellness risk, and a full doubling of decision autonomy.

Project goal

Give Eastbrook City a measured baseline, a monitoring tool, and six evidence-based intervention programs so the city can expand AI responsibly rather than blindly.

Problem Context

Three compounding problems, zero monitoring infrastructure

Young residents of Eastbrook increasingly rely on AI tools for everyday planning and decisions. The city's youth wellness strategy is at odds with this trend, and there is currently no system to measure, track, or address it.

🧠

Behavioral dependency

Youth show dramatically reduced critical thinking. When AI is unavailable, decision delay averages 85.6 seconds and collapses to 277.7 seconds at the highest reliance level. 32.4% of youth cannot decide independently at all.

👁️

Physical health impact

154 minutes of daily AI screen time is producing measurable consequences. 18.3% of youth show medium to high eye strain, and 30.5% report physical stiffness. Every high-strain case occurs above 200 minutes per day.

🏛️

Governance gap

Eastbrook has no monitoring system. City leadership cannot identify at-risk youth, validate interventions, or make evidence-based decisions about whether AI program expansion is safe to proceed.

Research Insights

Every intervention is grounded in published evidence

Four peer-reviewed sources informed the dataset variable design, the KPI formulas, and the six intervention programs. The Eastbrook dataset replicates several published findings in local data.

Walton Family Foundation · 2024

49% of students use ChatGPT weekly

This validates Daily_AI_Prompts at an average of 37.79 per day as the core behavioral monitoring variable. AI use at this scale is no longer exceptional — it is baseline behavior.

MDPI Societies · 2025 · N=666

AI use negatively correlates with critical thinking

The statistically significant finding directly supports the Decision Autonomy Rate KPI and the AI-Free Decision Zone intervention program. Eastbrook data shows the same pattern.

PLOS ONE · 2024 · N=512

63.7% of adolescents show digital eye strain

The empirical basis for the Physical Wellness Risk Index formula and the 90-minute daily screen limit guideline. The Eastbrook scatter plot confirms the same dose-response threshold above 200 minutes.

ACM CHI · 2023

Higher AI trust reduces verification behavior

The trust paradox is replicated in Eastbrook data. Trust Level 5 youth verify 1.67 times per day — less than Trust Level 4 at 2.18. The most trusting youth skip verification entirely.

Dataset Structure

1,500 profiles · 22 variables · 4 normalized tables

The dataset is synthetic, generated in Python using pandas and numpy. Variable ranges were calibrated against four published research benchmarks — not invented. The entity-relationship schema was designed in MySQL Workbench with four normalized relational tables connected through Youth_ID as the primary key.

Variable generation rules

Prompts

Range 0 to 100, average 37.79. Calibrated against Walton Foundation weekly frequency data. Higher reliance level increases prompt count. Younger age groups generate more.

Verification

Range 0 to 10, average 1.12. 35.4% of youth verify zero times. Left-skewed distribution designed to match ACM CHI trust-verification paradox findings.

Eye Strain

Ordinal 0 to 3. High strain (level 3) generated only when Screen_Time exceeds 200 minutes per day — directly replicating the PLOS ONE dose-response threshold.

Decision Delay

Range 0 to 600 seconds. Mean rises from 58.9s at Level 1 to 277.7s at Level 5 — modeled on Springer 2025 reliance-delay correlation data.

Reliance Level

Ordinal 1 to 5. Distribution: 988 at L1, 371 at L2, 107 at L3, 31 at L4, 3 at L5. Calibrated to reflect a realistic population curve, not a uniform distribution.

Stiffness

Binary yes/no. Probability increases with Screen_Time duration and reliance level. 30.5% report stiffness, consistent with Frontiers in Public Health 2022 findings.

The 6 Intervention Programs

Program 01

AI Literacy Program

Weekly school curriculum teaching youth when and how to verify AI outputs before acting on them. Directly targets the 1,001 youth who verify once or less per day.

Trigger: ADS > 12 or Verification < 2/day
Program 02

Fact-Check Challenge

Gamified daily exercises requiring youth to cross-reference AI outputs against trusted sources before acting on recommendations.

Trigger: Verification Frequency < 1.5/day
Program 03

Screen Time Guidelines

City-recommended 90-minute daily cap for AI screen use for youth under 18. The scatter plot in the data dashboard provides the direct evidence base for this number.

Trigger: PWRI > 0.25 or Screen > 180 min
Program 04

AI-Free Decision Zones

Timed class periods where AI tools are disabled, rebuilding autonomous thinking for the 486 youth with confirmed cognitive dependency.

Trigger: DAR < 1.5 or Decision Delay > 90s
Program 05

Counselor Alert System

Monthly dashboard reports flagging Level 4 and 5 youth for one-on-one digital wellness check-ins. Currently 141 youth qualify.

Trigger: AI Reliance Level 4 or higher
Program 06

Monthly Progress Tracking

The dashboard is remeasured monthly. City leadership sees whether KPIs improve and adjusts programs accordingly. Evidence governs the solution.

Trigger: All KPIs — continuous monitoring
Metrics for Success

Three computed KPIs from the dataset

Formulas were developed with GenAI assistance and adapted to the Eastbrook dataset variables, consistent with IS 5960 methodology. All three scores are live in the solution prototype and update when filters change.

High — Needs Intervention
KPI 1 — ADS
AI Dependence Score
14.29
unverified prompts per screen-hour · target below 8.0
ADS = (P − V) / (S ÷ 60)

P = prompts created per day (37.79) · V = verification frequency (1.12) · S = screen time in minutes (154). A score of 14.29 means a youth acts on 14 unverified AI outputs for every hour of screen use.

Moderate — Watch
KPI 2 — PWRI
Physical Wellness Risk Index
0.29
composite risk score (0 = no risk · 1 = critical) · target below 0.15
PWRI = (E/3 × 0.5) + (St × 0.3) + (S/480 × 0.2)

E = eye strain level 0 to 3 (weighted at 50%) · St = stiffness binary (30%) · S = screen time (20%). Current score of 0.29 places Eastbrook youth in the moderate risk zone.

Low — Critical Gap
KPI 3 — DAR
Decision Autonomy Rate
1.50
autonomy score · target above 3.0
DAR = (D × C) / (1 + Del ÷ 60)

D = can decide without AI (67.6%) · C = confidence score (avg 5.4/10) · Del = decision delay in seconds. A score of 1.50 is half the functional autonomy threshold. The target after interventions is above 3.0.

Ethical Considerations

Responsible by design

Four ethical dimensions were considered throughout the project and are embedded in the design of the dashboard and the intervention programs.

🔒

Privacy by design

All 1,500 records are fully synthetic. No real youth data was collected, stored, or analyzed at any point. Variable ranges were calibrated against published public research only.

⚖️

Algorithmic transparency

The Youth Assessment risk calculator produces algorithmic scores — not clinical assessments. This distinction is explicitly labeled in the interface to prevent misuse by counselors or city administrators.

🌍

Equity and inclusion

All six intervention programs are universally accessible to Eastbrook youth regardless of socioeconomic background. The awareness campaign reaches parents across all city neighborhoods equally.

🤝

Balance, not restriction

The project promotes balanced and critical AI use — not AI avoidance. The goal is to strengthen youth agency and independent thinking alongside AI engagement, not restrict beneficial technology access.

Lessons Learned

What the team learned this semester

Data and visualization must match

Early submissions contained written analysis values that did not match dashboard charts — caught in professor feedback. Rebuilding the dashboard in HTML with dataset constants embedded directly eliminated this permanently. Every number in every deliverable now traces to the same 1,500 rows.

KPI design starts from variables, not ideals

The three KPI formulas required working backward from available variables rather than forward from ideal metrics. Multiple GenAI-assisted iterations were needed before producing scores that were both mathematically valid and meaningful to a non-technical city stakeholder.

Adaptive execution under constraint

The mid-semester shift from Power BI to HTML required rapid role adjustment. The team learned that adaptive project management is not a contingency plan — it is a core professional skill. Precision in language matters as much as precision in data when building evidence for governance.
