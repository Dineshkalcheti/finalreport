Executive Summary

The project in brief

Eastbrook City planned to expand AI programs for youth aged 13–24 but had no data on whether current AI use was healthy or harmful. MRP Team 18 built the Youth AI Impact Monitoring Dashboard to fill this evidence gap.

Analysis of 1,500 synthetic youth profiles reveals that 64.8% of daily decisions are AI-assisted, verification rate averages just 1.12 out of 10, and 486 youth cannot decide independently. Average daily screen time is 154 minutes, producing documented physical harm.

Three computed KPIs quantify the severity: ADS = 14.29, PWRI = 0.29, DAR = 1.50. The solution — a publicly accessible HTML dashboard — monitors all indicators, triggers six intervention programs, and models a 44% ADS reduction, 48% PWRI drop, and 100% DAR improvement after 12 months.

Problem Context

Three compounding problems, zero monitoring

Young Eastbrook residents increasingly use AI for everyday decisions. The city has no system to measure, track, or address the resulting behavioral and physical harm.

🧠

Behavioral dependency

32.4% of youth cannot decide without AI. Decision delay escalates from 58.9s at Level 1 to 277.7s at Level 5. Confidence collapses from 4.32 to 1.33 out of 5. Verification averages 1.12 out of 10.

👁️

Physical health harm

18.3% show medium to high eye strain. 30.5% report body stiffness. Every high eye strain case occurs above 200 minutes of screen time per day — a clear, evidence-based threshold.

🏛️

Governance gap

Eastbrook currently has no monitoring infrastructure. City leadership cannot identify at-risk youth, validate interventions, or make evidence-based decisions about AI program expansion.

Proposed Solution

Youth AI Impact Monitoring System

A publicly accessible, no-login interactive HTML dashboard that directly addresses each dimension of the problem — behavioral, physical, and governance — through live monitoring, computed KPI scores, and targeted intervention programs.

🛠️
Solution Prototype App — Live and publicly accessible
7 functional pages · Age and tool filters that update every chart live · 22-variable risk calculator · Intervention trigger system showing exactly which youth qualify for each program
Open Solution App

How the app addresses each problem

🧠

Solves behavioral dependency

The KPI Dashboard page shows the AI Dependence Score (ADS = 14.29) and Decision Autonomy Rate (DAR = 1.50) with trend charts by age group and reliance level. Counselors can filter to Teens 13–17 and see exactly where dependency is highest. The Youth Assessment calculator flags individual youth who need the AI Literacy Program or AI-Free Decision Zones.

👁️

Solves physical health monitoring

The Physical Wellness page shows the scatter plot confirming the 200-minute eye strain threshold, stiffness rates by tool type, and the screen time histogram. The PWRI score (0.29) is displayed with its formula so administrators understand exactly what drives it. Youth exceeding 180 min/day are automatically flagged for Screen Time Guidelines.

🏛️

Solves the governance gap

The Interventions page shows live counts of how many youth trigger each of the 6 programs — clicking any card shows the exact list of youth and their metrics. The Risk Profiles page provides a sortable 1,500-row table. Monthly Progress Tracking keeps KPIs updated. City leadership finally has the monitoring infrastructure they lacked.

The 6 Intervention Programs

Program 01

AI Literacy Program

Weekly curriculum teaching youth how to verify AI outputs before acting. Targets the 1,001 youth who verify once or less per day.

Trigger: ADS > 12 OR Verification < 2/day
Program 02

Fact-Check Challenge

Gamified daily exercises requiring cross-referencing AI outputs against trusted sources.

Trigger: Verification_Frequency < 1.5/day
Program 03

Screen Time Guidelines

City-recommended 90-minute daily cap for AI screen use. Scatter plot confirms all high eye strain above 200 min/day.

Trigger: PWRI > 0.25 OR Screen > 180 min
Program 04

AI-Free Decision Zones

Timed class periods with AI disabled — rebuilds autonomous thinking for the 486 dependent youth.

Trigger: DAR < 1.5 OR Delay > 90 sec
Program 05

Counselor Alert System

Monthly reports flagging Level 4–5 youth for 1-on-1 digital wellness check-ins. 34 youth currently qualify.

Trigger: AI_Reliance_Level ≥ 4
Program 06

Monthly Progress Tracking

Dashboard remeasured monthly. City leadership reviews KPI improvements and adjusts program intensity.

Trigger: All KPIs — continuous monitoring

Benefits of the solution

Zero cost, zero login

Built on GitHub Pages with HTML and Chart.js — no server, no subscription, no authentication barrier. Any Eastbrook official opens it in their browser.

Evidence-driven decisions

Every intervention is triggered by a real KPI threshold, not policy assumptions. City leadership acts on measured data, not guesses.

Individual + population level

Works at the city population level (all 1,500 youth) and at the individual level (22-variable risk calculator for any single youth).

Live filter governance

Filter by age group or AI tool — every chart and KPI score updates instantly. Isolate the most at-risk segments in seconds.

Potential improvements on KPIs by implementing the solution

ADS
AI Dependence Score: 14.29 → below 8.0 (−44%)

The AI Literacy Program and Fact-Check Challenge raise daily verification frequency from 1.12 to 4.0/day. This directly shrinks the (P−V) numerator in the ADS formula by approximately 42 units, cutting the score by 44%. Real-time data collection via school APIs would enable weekly rather than monthly threshold triggers, accelerating this improvement.

PWRI
Physical Wellness Risk Index: 0.29 → below 0.15 (−48%)

Screen Time Guidelines reduce daily screen use from 154 to under 100 minutes, simultaneously lowering all three PWRI formula components — eye strain, stiffness, and screen time. If a mobile companion app allowed youth to self-report physical symptoms daily, intervention thresholds could trigger faster, compressing the improvement timeline from 12 months to 6.

DAR
Decision Autonomy Rate: 1.50 → above 3.0 (+100%)

AI-Free Decision Zones grow the proportion who can decide independently from 67.6% to 85%, raise confidence scores, and cut decision delay from 85.6 to under 50 seconds. Longitudinal cohort tracking across academic years would enable individual-level before/after measurement, strengthening evidence beyond population aggregates and allowing counselors to track each youth's recovery trajectory.

Metrics for Success

Three computed KPIs from the dataset

Formulas developed with GenAI assistance and adapted to Eastbrook dataset variables, consistent with IS 5960 methodology. All three update live in the solution app when filters change.

⚠ HIGH — Needs Intervention
KPI 1 — ADS
AI Dependence Score
14.29
unverified prompts per screen-hour · target < 8.0
ADS = (P − V) / (S ÷ 60)

P = Daily_AI_Prompts (37.79) · V = Verification_Frequency (1.12) · S = Screen_Interaction_Time in minutes (154)

⚠ MODERATE — Watch
KPI 2 — PWRI
Physical Wellness Risk Index
0.29
composite risk 0→1 scale · target < 0.15
PWRI = (E/3×0.5)+(St×0.3)+(S/480×0.2)

E = Eye_Strain_Level 0–3 (50% weight) · St = Physical_Stiffness binary (30%) · S = screen time (20%)

🔴 CRITICAL — Major Gap
KPI 3 — DAR
Decision Autonomy Rate
1.50
autonomy score · target > 3.0
DAR = (D × C) / (1 + Del ÷ 60)

D = Decision_Without_AI binary (67.6%) · C = Decision_Confidence 1–5 · Del = Decision_Delay_Without_AI in sec

Ethical Considerations

Responsible by design

🔒

Privacy by design

All 1,500 records are fully synthetic. No real youth data was collected or stored at any point. Variable ranges calibrated against public research only.

⚖️

Algorithmic transparency

The Youth Assessment calculator produces algorithmic scores — not clinical assessments. Explicitly labeled in the dashboard interface throughout.

🌍

Equity and inclusion

All 6 programs are universally accessible regardless of socioeconomic background. The awareness campaign reaches all city neighborhoods equally.

🤝

Balance, not restriction

The project promotes balanced AI use — not AI avoidance. The goal is to strengthen youth agency alongside AI engagement.

Lessons Learned

What the team learned

Data and visuals must match

Early submissions had analysis values that did not match dashboard charts — flagged by Dr. Cardona. Rebuilding in HTML with embedded dataset constants made every number auditable and consistent across all deliverables.

KPI design starts from variables

Formulas required working backward from available variables rather than forward from ideal metrics. Multiple iterations were needed before ADS, PWRI, and DAR were both mathematically valid and interpretable by a non-technical stakeholder.

Adaptive execution under constraint

The mid-semester transition from Power BI to HTML required rapid role adjustment. Precision in language (prompts created by youth, not received) matters as much as precision in data when building governance evidence.
